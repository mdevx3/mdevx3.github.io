01 Problem

Bridging the communication gap for the deaf community

Deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals face significant communication barriers in everyday interactions — from healthcare appointments to workplace conversations. Most people do not know ASL (American Sign Language), and professional interpreters are costly and not always available.

The goal was to build a real-time system capable of translating ASL hand gestures into readable text, operating at video frame rate without specialised hardware — making it accessible as a lightweight software tool.

02 Process

Combining landmark detection with deep learning classification

  • Used Google Mediapipe to extract 21 3D hand landmark keypoints per frame from the live webcam feed — providing a structured, low-dimensional representation of hand pose that is robust to lighting and skin tone variation.
  • Trained a TensorFlow deep learning model on a labelled gesture dataset, mapping sequences of landmark coordinates to ASL letter/word classes.
  • Integrated the full pipeline with OpenCV for real-time video capture and frame-by-frame inference, overlaying the predicted text label on the live video stream.
  • Built a Java-based frontend component for the user interface, connecting to the Python inference backend over a local bridge.
  • Validated accuracy using held-out gesture samples and iterated on the model architecture to improve classification confidence on ambiguous hand shapes.

The system achieved real-time ASL-to-text translation operating at live video frame rate on a standard laptop. The Mediapipe + TensorFlow architecture proved highly effective — landmark-based input made the model invariant to background and lighting conditions that would challenge pixel-based approaches.

The project earned one of the top scores in the course, demonstrating both the technical soundness of the approach and its practical accessibility impact. It also served as a strong foundation for exploring multi-word gesture sequences and continuous sign language recognition as future extensions.
